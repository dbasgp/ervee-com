Drainage·7 min read·February 2026

Condensate drainage: head, pumps, and code compliance.

A 145 L/day commercial ceiling unit can produce six litres an hour at peak. The drainage path needs to be as carefully designed as the duct path. Get either wrong and you have built a leak.

DBA's ceiling dehumidifiers use one of two drainage methods. The method is fixed by the unit, not the project. Specify the method correctly at the M&E stage and there is nothing left to design around.

Single-phase ceiling units: 1.8 m integrated pump

Every single-phase ceiling model — UTC20, UTC68, UTC120, plus the single-phase commercial GEC68LD-HP and GEC145LD-HP — ships with an integrated condensate pump rated for 1.8 m of head. The pump handles the lift; gravity handles the rest of the route.

Correct routing: rise to a high point, then fall

The drain line should leave the unit and rise vertically to a high point at or near the 1.8 m head limit. From that high point, the line falls by gravity to the discharge — typically an existing condensate stack or FCU drain.

The high point is an inverted-U at the top of the run. It serves two purposes:

  • It breaks any back-siphon from the discharge end — water cannot flow upstream into the unit
  • The downward gravity section can be as long as the building requires, without further pump load

Dropping the line straight down from the unit and trying to traverse it horizontally is the most common install mistake. Without a high-point break, the line is exposed to back-siphon and the pump fights gravity unnecessarily.

Three-phase ceiling units: gravity fall

The larger three-phase ceiling models — GEC280LD, GEC400LD, GEC550LD — have no integrated pump. Drainage is gravity-only. The line must slope continuously downward from the unit to the discharge with no rising section.

This constraint should be confirmed during the M&E coordination stage. A three-phase ceiling unit must be sited where a continuous fall to the discharge is physically possible.

Discharge point

Both methods discharge into an existing condensate route the building already supports — a stack, a floor trap, or an FCU drain. Treat the dehumidifier's drain the same way the project treats fan-coil condensate. Local plumbing codes apply.

Commissioning

Before sign-off:

  1. Run the unit in dehumidify mode for 30 minutes and verify water reaches the discharge
  2. For pumped units, observe pump cycling — short bursts spaced apart are normal; continuous running indicates a route problem
  3. Inspect the ceiling void after 24 hours of operation — it should be dry

For project-specific drainage queries, the engineering team will sketch a working route from a floor plan and section.
