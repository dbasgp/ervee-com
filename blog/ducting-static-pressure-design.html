Ducting·8 min read·January 2026

Static pressure budgets for ducted dehumidifiers.

A ceiling dehumidifier's fan has a limit. Burn more pressure on duct, bends, and grilles than the fan can deliver, and the unit quietly under-performs forever without ever throwing a fault.

Most "this unit is not pulling its rated capacity" tickets come down to a fan that ran out of static pressure. The compressor is fine, the refrigerant charge is correct, the sensor is honest — the fan is just not pushing enough air across the coil for the rated extraction. Specifying the duct properly at design time avoids the problem entirely.

The fan curve in plain language

Each unit has a maximum external static pressure (ESP) — the resistance the fan can overcome and still deliver rated airflow. DBA's commercial ranges publish these on the datasheet:

ModelAirflow at rated ESPMax ESP
DBA-UTC68500 CMH30 Pa
DBA-UTC120890 CMH40 Pa
DBA-GEC68LD-HP500 CMH80 Pa
DBA-GEC145LD-HP1,200 CMH120 Pa
DBA-GEC280LD1,700 CMH150 Pa

Stay below the max ESP and you get rated capacity. Go over and airflow drops; the coil sees less air; less moisture is removed per hour; the room stalls above setpoint.

Budgeting the loss

Add up the static pressure cost of every component between the unit and the served zone:

  • Insulated flexible duct: 1.5–2.5 Pa/m at typical airflows
  • Rigid spiral duct: 0.5–1.0 Pa/m
  • 90° bend in flex (sharp): 10–15 Pa
  • 90° bend in rigid (long radius): 3–5 Pa
  • Supply grille at design flow: 5–15 Pa depending on style
  • Return grille + filter (clean): 15–25 Pa
  • Return grille + filter (loaded): 40–60 Pa

Always design with the filter at its dirty-end pressure, not its clean-day reading. A clean install that hits its capacity number perfectly on day one will slip into trouble six months later if the budget was set on a fresh filter.

A worked example

A DBA-UTC120 (40 Pa ESP) serving an open-plan office. Trunk is 4 m of 250 mm flex with three 90° bends. Two branches at 200 mm flex, each 3 m with two bends, ending in a 4-way diffuser. Return is 600 × 200 mm via a filtered grille.

Budget:

  • Trunk friction: 4 m × 2 Pa/m = 8 Pa
  • Trunk bends: 3 × 12 Pa = 36 Pa — already over budget

This will not work. Either change the trunk to rigid (4 m × 0.75 Pa/m + 3 × 4 Pa = 15 Pa), or pick a unit with higher ESP. A DBA-GEC68LD-HP at 80 Pa ESP is the cleaner solution if rated capacity is met by 68 L/day instead of 120 L/day.

Sizing the duct cross-section

Keep design velocity below 4 m/s. Above that you trade pressure loss and noise for nothing useful. Convert airflow (CMH) to velocity at a given diameter, and step up the duct until you are under 4 m/s. As a quick guide, the right minimum diameter for round duct:

AirflowMinimum diameter
500 CMH200 mm
900 CMH250 mm
1,200 CMH300 mm
1,700 CMH350 mm

Designing for balance

When a trunk feeds two or more branches, air takes the path of least resistance. Two branches of equal length and grille type self-balance to roughly equal flow. Unequal branches do not. Fit a manual damper on every branch; balance by reading flow at each diffuser with an anemometer at commissioning.

The 70-90% rule

Aim for total calculated static pressure between 70% and 90% of the unit's max ESP. Below 70% you bought more fan than you needed. Above 90% you are buying performance loss as filters age.
