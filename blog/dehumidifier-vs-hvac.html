Dehumidifier vs HVAC: separating sensible from latent.

Both rely on a vapour-compression cycle. They are sized for completely different jobs. Mixing them up is why offices in Lagos, Mumbai, Houston, and Manila feel cold and clammy at the same time.

Two flavours of heat

The total cooling load on a building has two components. Sensible heat is what a thermometer reads — the temperature you need to drop to make the room feel cool. Latent heat is the energy stored in water vapour — what you have to remove to lower humidity. In tropical or coastal climates the latent fraction can exceed the sensible fraction. Lagos in July sees outside air around 30°C / 90% RH; more than half the energy needed to dry and cool that air to 23°C / 55% RH is latent.

The Sensible Heat Ratio (SHR) of equipment tells you how the unit splits its work:

  • A typical packaged DX air conditioner: SHR around 0.75. For every 1 kW of cooling delivered, 0.75 kW is sensible (temperature reduction) and 0.25 kW is latent (moisture removal).
  • A dedicated dehumidifier: SHR near 0.05 (it returns slightly warmer air with much lower moisture).
  • A humid outdoor air load: SHR around 0.45 — meaning more than half of what the building has to do is latent.

An air conditioner sized to handle the building's sensible load and incidentally remove some moisture cannot do both well when the latent fraction is that high. The compressor cycles off when temperature hits setpoint, the coil warms up, and moisture removal stops while the room still feels damp.

Why oversized HVAC makes humidity worse

This is the most common diagnosis in tropical commercial buildings. The engineer sizes the chiller plant for the peak sensible load — that is, the hottest hour of the year. For 99% of operating hours, that capacity is unused. The system short-cycles. Each cycle is too brief for the coil to stay cold long enough to condense significant moisture. The room reads 22°C and 70% RH and the occupants complain.

The fix is not a bigger air conditioner. It is a smaller air conditioner plus a dedicated dehumidifier — what the industry calls a Dedicated Outdoor Air System (DOAS) when applied to the fresh-air stream. The dehumidifier handles latent; the AC handles sensible. Both run inside their efficiency sweet spot.

Energy maths for a commercial floor

Take a 500 m² office in Jakarta. Sensible load 35 kW. Latent load 25 kW (heavy occupancy + fresh-air component). Total 60 kW.

  • AC-only solution: 60 kW chiller-fed FCU array running at SHR 0.75 to satisfy sensible. To satisfy latent it has to oversize to 90 kW or run far below setpoint temperature. Either route is 25–35% more energy than needed.
  • Dehumidifier + AC solution: 35 kW FCU array on sensible duty, plus four DBA-GEC145LD-HP ceiling units handling 580 L/day latent. AC compressor runtime drops by roughly 35%, latent load is handled where it is most efficient to handle, occupant comfort goes up.

The capital cost of the second route is higher upfront but pays back in 3–4 years on energy alone — sooner where electricity tariffs are high.

Residential markets

The same principle applies at smaller scale. A typical 2 HP residential air conditioner in tropical Asia is dramatically oversized for the room it serves. Adding a small ceiling dehumidifier (DBA-UTC68 size) lets the homeowner run the AC at 26°C in dry air, which feels like 22°C in wet air. Bills drop; clothes in wardrobes stop developing mildew; the room feels properly comfortable instead of just cold.

The decision rule

If outdoor air is above 28°C and 70% RH for more than a third of the year, dedicated dehumidification will outperform AC-only. Below that threshold, AC-only may be enough. The map of "tropical and coastal" mostly tells you which side of the line you are on.

What this means for distributors

For trade partners selling into these climates, the dehumidifier conversation is not "instead of an AC" — it is "alongside the AC, sized for the latent load." That framing avoids the false dichotomy and matches what owners actually experience: aircon that gets the room cold but never quite comfortable.
