Sizing dehumidifiers by application, not by floor area.

A 500 m² warehouse and a 500 m² wine cellar have nothing in common except the floor area. One needs 3 L/day of moisture removal. The other needs 50. Sizing tables that average them are wrong for both.

Real moisture load is the sum of every gram of water that enters the conditioned volume per hour. The four sources — infiltration, occupants, processes, and stored material — are weighted completely differently in each application. Here is how we size for the most common cases.

Warehouses and logistics

The dominant load is infiltration through dock doors. A 5,000 m² ambient-temperature warehouse with a dozen dock doors cycling all day in a humid climate can take in 200–400 L/day of moisture from outside air alone. Stored goods (cardboard, paper, hygroscopic powders) contribute a much smaller daily load but add a one-time conditioning load on first occupation.

Method: count dock-door open-hours per day, multiply by the dock-area infiltration rate (typically 8–15 kg/hr per m² of open area for warm humid markets), and add a base infiltration of 0.1 air changes per hour for the rest of the envelope. A 5,000 m² × 6 m warehouse at 25°C target / 60% RH in a 30°C / 80% RH climate sits around 250–350 L/day.

Recommended: 2× DBA-GE400LD-HP or 3× DBA-GE280LD-HP depending on the layout. Mount along the dock wall so dehumidified air sweeps inward.

Wine cellars and spirits storage

Tight RH (55–65%) and stable temperature (12–16°C) are non-negotiable. Floor area is misleading — a 50 m² wine room with 15,000 bottles has high thermal mass but low moisture sources. The challenge is precision, not capacity.

Method: calculate latent load from infiltration only (occupancy is sporadic). For a 50 m² well-sealed wine room in a tropical climate, expect 4–8 L/day load at the target RH. Step up significantly if there are exposed timber racks (which release moisture during humid spells).

Recommended: DBA-HC68 or HC145 humidity control unit. The humidity control range can also humidify — important because the room can drift below 55% RH in dry seasons, which dries out cork.

Lithium battery production and storage

The strictest application. Manufacturing requires dew points below −40°C in dry rooms; storage typically 15–25% RH at room temperature. Refrigerant dehumidifiers cannot reach these targets — desiccant rotors are the only option.

Method: total air change rate × ambient water content × safety factor. For a 200 m² dry room targeting 1% RH at 22°C, expect 8–15 kg/hr moisture removal duty after the room is established (much higher during initial dry-down).

Recommended: DBA-DD850 with appropriate pre-treatment, often paired with a refrigerant pre-dryer to drop ambient air to 50% RH before the desiccant stage. These are project-grade systems we design with the client.

Indoor swimming pools

Evaporation from the water surface is the load. A 30 m² unheated pool in a 28°C indoor environment evaporates 80–120 L/day; a heated 30 m² pool may exceed 200 L/day. Pool water chemistry (chlorine, salt) also drives a corrosion specification — use units with coated coils and stainless drain pans.

Method: water surface area × empirical evaporation rate (0.1–0.2 kg/hr per m² for pool surfaces), increased by activity. Indoor pools at hotels add 50% for swimmer movement.

Recommended: DBA-GE550LD-HP with corrosion-resistant package for commercial pools; multiple smaller commercial GEC units for residential.

Post-flood restoration

Different from any of the above. A flooded building absorbs 50–200 L per affected room into walls, floors, ceilings, and contents. Capacity needs to be massive for the first 3–7 days to drive a controlled dry-down.

Method: affected floor area × 5 L/day for 5 days, plus contents weight × 0.1 L/kg. A 100 m² flooded office is roughly 500 L over 5 days, or 100 L/day capacity.

Recommended: 2× DBA-GEX110 or 4× DBA-GEX75 portable LGR units, deployed and run continuously. After the bulk of the water is out (day 3–5), reduce capacity by 50% to avoid overdrying walls and floors.

The principle behind it

Sizing is always about matching capacity to load, and load is determined by what is happening in the room, not how big it is. Same floor area, different application — different unit. Start from the source list, not the floor plan.

For projects in any of these categories, we run sizing calculations free for distributor partners. Send the application, ambient and target conditions, room volume, and any process notes — usually a paragraph and a sketch is enough.
