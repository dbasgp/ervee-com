Installing a ceiling dehumidifier across markets.

The unit ships the same way wherever it goes. The install around it does not. Here is what changes between Europe, the Middle East, North America, and the tropics — and the briefing notes worth handing to a local M&E partner before they pop the ceiling.

What is the same everywhere

Every DBA ceiling dehumidifier ships pre-charged with refrigerant, with the integrated drain pump, sensors, and BMS hardware factory-tested. The fundamental install sequence is the same across markets:

  1. Survey the ceiling void for clearance, structural anchor points, and drain destinations
  2. Suspend the unit on threaded rod with vibration isolators
  3. Connect ducting, balance dampers, and supply / return grilles
  4. Route drainage with the correct head and slope
  5. Energise via a dedicated circuit with a local isolator
  6. Position the remote humidity sensor and wire BMS / dry contacts
  7. Commission, verify drainage, and walk the served zones

What changes is the regulatory and material context around each of these steps.

Voltage and frequency

DBA's UTC and smaller GEC ceiling units run on 220–240 V 50 Hz single-phase as standard. Larger GEC and all GE industrial units use 380 V 3-phase 50 Hz. Two caveats for projects outside the SEA region:

  • North America: 60 Hz at 208/230 V. Standard ceiling units run on 50 Hz factory wiring; specify the 60 Hz variant at order time. Three-phase 480 V conversion is available on the industrial range with a documented build code.
  • Middle East: 400 V 3-phase 50 Hz with TT or TN-S earthing. DBA ships these directly without modification, but local code may require a 30 mA Type-A RCD upstream of the supply circuit; this is not factory-fitted.
  • Europe (CE markets): 230 V single-phase / 400 V 3-phase 50 Hz, factory-wired matches. CE-marked units ship with EU-compliant labelling and Declaration of Conformity.

Always confirm voltage and frequency on the order, not after the unit lands.

Ducting standards

The unit's duct flange is a metric spigot in mm. Adapters are needed for:

  • Imperial duct (USA, Canada, parts of UK refurb): 8" / 10" / 12" rigid round duct. Use a metric-to-imperial transition piece. The pressure loss is negligible at correctly sized transitions.
  • Rectangular sheet metal duct (US commercial): a round-to-rectangular transition placed within 600 mm of the unit's spigot, with at least a 30° taper to avoid pressure loss.
  • Pre-insulated flex duct (European residential): direct compatibility with metric flange; use the recommended fastening clamps and foil tape.

Static pressure loss budgets, regardless of region, follow the same physics — see our ducting design notes for the calculation.

Drainage

Two methods, by unit:

  • Single-phase ceiling units (UTC and GEC68/145LD-HP): integrated condensate pump rated 1.8 m head, with a check valve built into the supplied drain hose. The line rises from the unit to a high point at or near the pump limit, then falls by gravity to the discharge — using the full lift and letting gravity carry the rest.
  • Three-phase ceiling units (GEC280LD and above): no pump. Drainage is gravity-only — the line must slope continuously downward from the unit with no rising section.

Building-side discharge follows the local plumbing code for HVAC condensate — typically indirect discharge to an existing condensate route the building already supports. The full method is in our drainage notes.

BMS and controls

RS485 Modbus is the lingua franca. Every UTC, GEC, GE, HC, and DD model exposes it. Beyond that, the Modbus register map (provided in the commissioning packet) maps to whichever BMS the building uses — Tridium Niagara, Honeywell, Siemens Desigo, JCI Metasys, generic BACnet via gateway. The only region-specific note is that some Middle East commercial buildings require a hard-wired Class-1 isolating barrier between the BMS network and the unit; we supply the spec on request.

WiFi App control on smaller UTC and GEC68 units works on standard 2.4 GHz 802.11 b/g/n. App availability extends to both iOS and Android worldwide.

Vibration and noise expectations

Residential expectations differ. EU and North American residential clients tend to be more sensitive to compressor hum at 35–40 dB than tropical-market clients used to FCU noise. For installs above a bedroom or above a quiet office, specify:

  • Spring-mounted suspension instead of rigid isolators
  • An acoustic lining in the immediate plenum (5–10 mm closed-cell foam)
  • A flexible duct connection at the supply, not rigid spiral

These three changes can drop the perceived noise by 6–8 dB — the difference between "I notice it" and "I forgot it was there."

Briefing note for local M&E

Provide the local contractor with: unit datasheet, voltage / frequency confirmation, duct spigot dimensions, condensate pump head and hose size, Modbus register map, and clearance dimensions for service access. Most install issues we are asked about come from one of these six being missed.

The container-load reality

Most international orders arrive by sea freight in 40-foot containers. The packaging is engineered for that journey — wooden crates with foam corner protection, internal moisture-absorbent sachets, and forklift-ready pallet bases. On unpacking, two checks before install:

  • Visually inspect the refrigeration tubing on each unit for crush damage
  • Confirm the desiccant sachet inside the casing is intact and dry

If either fails, photograph and report within 7 days — we ship replacements covered under our worldwide warranty.
